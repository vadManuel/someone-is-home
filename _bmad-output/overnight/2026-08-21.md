## Unit 1 — the flow spine: a walkable screen graph in `:ui`

**Outcome: done, green, pushed.** Commit `4fdaaaa` — *"The screen list learns what may follow
what"*. Full `./gradlew check`: **tests=364,
failures=0** across every module and both targets (desktop and `iosSimulatorArm64`), up from 344
— 20 new. `DeviceLayoutTest` stayed green throughout (56 screens × 2 roles against a faked
notch), and `:ui:boundaryCheck` still reports `ui clean` — the flow layer sees no `:core`.

### What it is

`ScreenId` has always been *a screen list, not a navigation graph* — it says what exists and
nothing about what may follow what. `ui/src/commonMain/kotlin/home/someoneshome/ui/Flow.kt` is
the other half:

- **`ScreenGraph.exitsOf`** — what each screen's own controls reach, as an exhaustive `when` over
  `ScreenId`, so adding a screen without deciding what it reaches is a compile error rather than a
  screen with no way out. Same discipline as `Screen`'s own `when`.
- **`Flow.autoAdvance`** — the transitions the house makes with no tap, as a map. Boot's
  fall-through, the scan's ten-second window, the meeting from the ring to the lights going out,
  and the same meeting walked from outside the system.
- **`Flow.houseDriving`** — where stepping backwards is refused.
- **`Flow.housePushed` / `Flow.unrouted` / `Flow.viaActions`** — the three honest footnotes: the
  screens only the authority can reach, the two the port cannot reach at all, and the one edge the
  actions layer owns rather than a screen.
- **`FlowModel` + `FlowHost`** — the thing that walks it. `PanelState` stayed flat and inert; the
  trail sits beside it, is never read by a screen, and decides no game question.
- The playtest and debug roots now open on `Boot` and walk themselves. `CheatRoot`'s private
  local-state navigation is gone; the picker is now for the screens no walk reaches plus the role.

### The timings are placeholders, and that is the point

**Every number in `Flow.autoAdvance` is presentation timing.** In play the house owns the clock —
it decides when a ring is answered, when the talk starts, when the lights go out — and pushes the
screen. The table is what a phone with no house attached does instead, and it goes away when the
transport carries a round. Where the design already carried a number the table borrowed it rather
than inventing one; every row carries a `why` naming the cue on the screen that asked for it, and
a test fails a row that has none.

The one number that is *not* arbitrary is the scan window: `SCAN_SEGMENTS * 500` = ten seconds,
derived from the bar that draws it rather than restated beside it, so the countdown on screen and
the moment the light dies cannot drift apart.

### The transcription is read off the screens, not reviewed against them

`ScreenGraphTest` renders all 56 screens across both roles, both room types and markers on and
off, fires every tap target each one publishes via its click semantics action, and compares what
the screens asked for with what the graph claims. A retargeted button fails the build and names
both sides. It uses the semantics action rather than `performClick` deliberately: a pointer event
at a node's centre never reaches a control drawn underneath another one, so the graph would come
out *smaller* than the screen and agree with itself.

It passed on its first run against a graph transcribed from a throwaway probe of the same shape —
which is the result you want from a transcription, and worth nothing until it has been made to
fail (below).

### The no-back set is derived, not invented

D-102 ratified the haptic doctrine: *a screen that arrives unasked buzzes; a screen you tapped
into does not.* Read the other way round, a screen that buzzed is one the house put you on, and
nothing behind it is still true. So `houseDriving` is built from `PanelVals.BUZZING` minus the one
exception the doctrine itself names — the host's registration scan, which buzzes because the phone
is against a card with the display angled away — plus seven screens that buzz on nobody's phone
and are still the house's, each named with its reason at the declaration.

`PanelVals.buzzes` was a `setOf(...)` literal rebuilt on every construction of a `PanelVals`, i.e.
on every recomposition of every screen, to answer one membership question. It is now a
`companion object` constant. Against a whole-app budget of ~0.5 MB/s that is a small win on its
own; the reason it is here is that it makes the buzzing set a single source the flow layer can be
*derived* from rather than kept in step with by hand.

### What the tests actually hold

`FlowTest` (20, common — runs on desktop and on `iosSimulatorArm64`) proves the properties that
hold over the whole graph at once, which is exactly the kind nobody checks by walking a phone:

- every screen either offers a way onward or is one of six that are **waiting for the house**
  (revoked, restrained, outside the system, disconnected, and the two endings);
- starting from `Boot` plus the house-pushed screens, **every screen in the game is reached** — no
  orphans, nothing drawn and reachable by nothing;
- and its converse, that **nothing walks to a screen only the house can push**. That one bites: a
  tap wired straight to `Revoked` would let a client put itself out of the round, and the list
  would still say the house did it;
- back is refused on exactly the house's screens and works everywhere else, in both directions;
- the meeting is a line from any of the three ways it starts through to the lights going out;
- the two ways out are still not synonyms — walking onto an out screen carries the cause, so the
  carrier reads REVOKED or RESTRAINED rather than the UNREGISTERED fallback.

`FlowHostTest` (3, desktop) proves the *host* drives rather than the table being well-formed, on
the test clock so a ninety-second discussion is checked in microseconds.

### Decisions taken (minor, inside the established design language) — flagged, not silent

- **`RoomEdit`'s STAIRS chip now routes to `StairsWarn`, and the type does not change until the
  host answers.** Without it that screen is an orphan: nothing on any screen reaches it, because
  the chip calls `pickRoomType` rather than naming a target — the one place the port hands a
  navigation decision to the actions layer. The design's reason is D-099: stairs hold nothing, so
  turning an occupied room into one unregisters every card in it, and the host is told what that
  costs *before* it happens. Setting the type first and asking afterwards would be the destructive
  edit happening before the question about it. `Flow.viaActions` records it as an actions-layer
  edge rather than smuggling it into `ScreenGraph`, where it would be a claim about a control that
  does not exist. Confirming (UNREGISTER AND CONTINUE) sets the type and returns to the editor;
  the port's `confirmStairs` previously navigated without changing anything.
- **The port's held room always holds markers, so the warning is unconditional.** Whether a
  particular room holds anything is host-setup data that arrives with the state; an empty room
  should one day change type in place with nothing to warn about.
- **The trail is capped at 32.** A round is twenty-five minutes of tapping around a springboard,
  and a list that only ever grows is an allocation the whole-app budget pays for.
- **`arrivingAt` moved out of the cheats into `:ui`** and both callers now share it. A picker that
  landed on the revoked screen with no cause rendered UNREGISTERED and read as a bug in the chrome.

### Flagged for a person — not escalations, but somebody should look

- **`Ghost2` does not buzz.** A revoked player sitting in the dark is told to STAND AND WALK IN,
  on a near-black screen facing away from them, with no haptic. D-102's canonical set is twelve
  screens and `PanelVals.BUZZING` holds seventeen; `Ghost2` is in neither. A buzz is identical for
  every player so adding one is not a tell, but it is D-102's set and I did not touch it.
- **`TermTaken` and `NoTerminal` are unreachable — recorded in `Flow.unrouted`.** Both are
  host-setup refusals the host is meant to hit during the setup walk (a terminal card offered to a
  second room; a save attempted with no terminal anywhere), and both are stranded because the
  screen that ought to route to them names an unconditional target instead: `Editor`'s REVIEW HOME
  always goes to `SaveName` even when `saveLabel` reads NEEDS A TERMINAL, and `ScanMarker`'s DONE
  SCANNING always goes to `MarkerSheet`. Closing it is a change to those two screens, not to the
  flow layer, so it was left alone and written down.
- **`IntegrityWidget`'s KDoc is stale after D-103.** It still says *"28 of 32, thirty-two segments
  rather than a percentage"* while the widget it documents renders `88%`. The code is right and
  the comment describes the design that revision 21 superseded. Not touched — it is a one-line doc
  fix somebody should make deliberately rather than have an overnight unit make silently.
- **`PanelVals` builds its fixtures eagerly on every construction** — `inbox`, `ballots`,
  `roomMarkers`, `current` — which means once per recomposition of every screen. `buzzes` was
  fixed because the flow layer needed the set; the rest was left alone as out of unit. Worth a
  pass against the ~0.5 MB/s budget before there is a lamp on the same device.

## Unit 2 — the plan model: what the host drew, in `:model`

**Outcome: done, green, pushed.** Commit `34a7001` — *"The house learns its own shape"*. Full
`./gradlew check`: **tests=464, failures=0, errors=0** across every module and both targets, up

### What it is

The setup walk has two halves. `HouseMap` was the half that binds cards to rooms; `HousePlan` is
the half that says what the rooms **are**.

- `model/.../HousePlan.kt` — `Cell`, `CellRect`, `PlanRoom`, `Floor`, `HousePlan`, `PaintResult`.
  One grid plan per storey, rooms painted as unions of rectangular drags, named and tagged room or
  stairs. `paint` refuses politely so an editor can say why with the finger still on the screen;
  `HousePlan.of` `require`s the same things as the last-ditch guarantee for storage and every other
  path. Same shape as `HouseMap.register` and `Registration`'s `init` (D-099).
- `model/.../HousePlanText.kt` — format version 1, `someone-is-home/house-plan/1`. HouseMapText's
  doctrine applied line for line: versioned header checked rather than tolerated, every failure
  throwing `MalformedHousePlan` naming the line, nothing skipped, names escaped so a room called
  `KITCHEN|room|0,0,1,1` cannot forge a row.
- `platform/.../HousePlanStore.kt` + the iOS actual, beside `HouseMapStore`.
- `platform/.../DocumentStore.ios.kt` — the atomic write, the Documents path and the
  create-if-absent step, now written once instead of twice. `HouseMapStore.ios.kt` is rewired onto
  it and keeps the bug story that produced it in the shared file's KDoc.

### A room is a LIST of rects, not one rect

**This is a deliberate departure from the unit brief's wording, and it is the whole reason the
grid exists.** The GDD argues the grid over free rectangles on three counts, and the second is
that *L-shaped rooms just work, because real houses have them and rectangles cannot express them*.
A room modelled as a single rect deletes that quietly: an L-shaped kitchen becomes two rooms with
two names, and a player told to go to the kitchen has two places to stand — which is exactly the
`ShapeAlreadyRegistered` hazard D-086 refused, arriving through geometry instead. The **stroke**
is rectangular, because a drag is; the **room** is the union. E4's acceptance criterion —
*an L-shaped room is expressible* — has a test on it that reads the eight cells back.

The other two counts are paid in the same file. `Floor.neighboursOf` derives adjacency from cell
neighbours with no geometry (E4.9's *adjacency is queryable without geometry*), and there are no
resize handles, overlap rules or snapping anywhere in it.

### Decided here, and flagged rather than buried

1. **Adjacency is four-neighbourhood, not eight.** Two rooms meeting at one corner share no
   doorway; a diagonal neighbour would report a route through a wall, and error injection would
   place a player somewhere they could not have walked to. Injection-verified — adding the two
   diagonals fails `rooms touching only at a corner are not neighbours`.
2. **Adjacency never crosses a floor, stairs included.** *No vertical-connection logic* — the app
   renders what was drawn. A property test holds it.
3. **`Floor` carries no number.** Floors are purely additive, and an index is an ordering, which
   is one short step from "the stairs on floor 0 lead to floor 1" — the logic the design refused.
   Position in `floors` is the only order there is and means only the order they were added in.
4. **Room names are unique across the whole plan, not per floor.** A card is registered to a
   `Room`, which is a name and a kind — so two rooms called BATHROOM on different storeys give a
   registration two places it could mean, and the host who painted them is the only person who
   would ever know. Costs a host one rename during setup, in the light. **This is a judgement
   call and is the one most worth a second opinion.**
5. **`HousePlan.LIMIT = 256` cells.** A format sanity bound, **not a balance value** — a file
   claiming a stroke two million cells wide is corrupted, and expanding it to cells before finding
   that out is how a host's phone dies mid-setup. Roughly six times the widest plan anyone would
   paint by hand.
6. **Negative coordinates are ordinary.** A host who starts painting in the middle of the hall and
   works outwards must not hit an origin.
7. **A separate file, not a section of the map file.** The plan and the map are lost differently —
   a host repaints a wall without re-registering forty cards. One file means every save rewrites
   both, so a plan could be destroyed by a save that was only ever about a card. A test holds it.
8. **No `housePlanStorageDirectory()`.** There is one setup directory and `houseMapStorageDirectory`
   already names it.

### The bug the injection pass found

The reader tracked the open storey's name in a `var` starting at `""`. That sentinel is **also**
what a nameless floor row parses to, so `F ` was refused as *"two floors called ''"* by the
duplicate check before the empty-name check could speak. The right answer for the wrong reason,
and the reason is the half a host reads. It surfaced only because the empty-name guard came back
ASLEEP under injection — no test failed when it was disabled, because another guard was quietly
answering for it. The name and its rooms are now one nullable pair, and a test asserts the
message rather than only the refusal.

## Unit 3 — the editor, real: `HomeEditorModel` in `:ui` over U2's plan

**Outcome: done, green, pushed.** Commit `909f4cf` — *"Two corners and a name"*. Full
`./gradlew check`: **tests=525, failures=0, errors=0** across every module and both targets, up
from 464 — 61 new (26 editor tests running on both desktop and `iosSimulatorArm64`, 5 gesture
tests on desktop, 2 flow tests on both). Both lints green, `:ui:boundaryCheck` still reports
`ui clean`, `DeviceLayoutTest` green throughout.

### What it is

The host-setup screens were a picture of one bungalow: six rooms in a hardcoded rect list, six
label placements nudged by hand, "6 ROOMS . 5 MARKERS" written down, and a plan you could tap
anywhere on to reach a room panel that always said GARAGE. They are now an editor.

- `ui/HomeEditor.kt` — `HomeEditorModel`, the editing state, sitting **beside** `PanelState` the
  way `FlowModel`'s trail does. `PanelState` stayed flat and inert; a plan under a finger is
  neither flat nor decided. Nothing in it answers a game question and nothing can — it is
  host-side setup drawn in the light, saved, and never read during a round, which is what makes
  real mutable state lawful in a module that cannot see `:core`.
- `EditorSurface` — the grid under the finger. Drag two corners on bare grid to paint a room;
  **drag starting inside a room to add a stroke to it**, which is how an L-shaped room gets
  drawn. `FloorTab`s switch storeys, the labels and chips are read off the painted rooms, and
  the drag draws a live preview that goes to the caution colour when it would be refused.
- Every refusal `HousePlan.paint` can return is translated in one place, so a crossing drag says
  THAT CROSSES KITCHEN under the host's finger rather than doing nothing — which is
  indistinguishable from a missed touch.
- The room panel's five preset chips and the name field are the same call. A rename carries the
  room's cards and the T card with it.
- Floors are real: tabs switch, ADD A FLOOR adds one and opens it empty, and every count on
  Floors and Save Home is counted off the plan.
- REVIEW HOME asks the plan whether any room holds the T card, and goes to `NoTerminal` when
  none does.

### D-098 and D-099 surfaced rather than re-derived

D-098 already held structurally — `RoomKind` has carried exactly `Room` and `Stairs` since
revision 20 — so the type chips read that enum directly and `ui.RoomType`, which was a second
copy of it, is deleted. D-099 is enforced *in the change*: `setKind(Stairs)` unregisters the
room's cards as part of becoming stairs, not in a step that follows it. `PanelState.roomType`
and `PanelState.hasTerminal` went with it: the plan is where a room's type and the terminal are
written down, and a second copy on `PanelState` is a second copy that would one day disagree
with the plan it describes.

### The stairs warning is conditional now, which closes a unit-1 flag

Unit 1 recorded: *"The port's held room always holds markers, so the warning is unconditional…
an empty room should one day change type in place with nothing to warn about."* The plan knows
what a room holds, so it does. A host tagging a bare stairwell is no longer interrogated about
cards that were never there.

### `NoTerminal` is routed, which closes another

`Flow.unrouted` is down to `TermTaken` alone. `TermTaken` stays stranded for the same reason it
always was, now written down properly: it is the refusal for *scanning a second T card*, which
is story 4.5 and has no camera behind it yet.

### Two bugs the gesture test found, and nothing else could have

`EditorSurfaceTest` drives real touch input. It exists because `ScreenGraphTest` fires click
**semantics actions** and a `pointerInput` publishes none, and `DeviceLayoutTest` measures
pixels — so between them the entire painting gesture could have been wired to nothing with every
test green. Both of these were live in code that had already passed a full green `check`:

1. **`detectDragGestures` reports the position at which a gesture *became* a drag**, after touch
   slop. The anchor arrived a cell late: a four-cell room came out three cells, and a drag
   started inside a room made a *new* room beside it instead of growing it —
   `expected:<[Cell(x=4, y=0), Cell(x=4, y=1), Cell(x=4, y=2), Cell(x=4, y=3)]> but
   was:<[Cell(x=4, y=1), Cell(x=4, y=2), Cell(x=4, y=3)]>`. Rewritten with `awaitFirstDown`.
2. **`awaitFirstDown` defaults to `requireUnconsumed`**, and the tap detector is the inner node
   and consumes the down first. The drag then waits forever and paints nothing, silently — *"the
   drag painted nothing: null"*.

The model's own tests could not have caught either: they are *told* which cells the finger
crossed, so they were being told the truth about a lie.

### Decisions taken (minor, inside the established design language) — flagged, not silent

- **A drag that starts inside a room grows that room.** The design says only *drag two corners to
  add a room*, but a room is a list of strokes and the whole point of the grid is that L-shaped
  rooms work — an editor that could only ever make single-rect rooms deletes E4's acceptance
  criterion quietly. Starting inside a room is unambiguous: a drag from bare grid can only be a
  new room, and a second room where one already is would overlap. The editor's hint gained a
  third line, DRAG FROM INSIDE ONE TO GROW IT, or it is undiscoverable.
- **A tap on the plan is an actions-layer edge** (`Flow.viaActions[Editor] = {RoomEdit}`), and
  `ScreenGraph.exitsOf(Editor)` no longer claims `RoomEdit`. Where a tap lands depends on which
  room is under it, and on most of a grid there is no room and it lands nowhere — a screen cannot
  name a target that is a position. Walked by a test, like the other two.
- **`NoTerminal`'s OPEN A ROOM goes to `Editor`, not `RoomEdit`.** The host has to choose *which*
  room the terminal goes in — *put it somewhere awkward* is the load-bearing line — and only the
  plan can ask that.
- **The editor draws no held room; the room panel does.** The panel always holds a room so it
  never opens on nothing, but an editor that lit the held one would open on a house with a room
  already picked out, reading as a selection the host did not make. Confirmed by screenshot: the
  first render had KITCHEN dark for no reason a host could explain.
- **New rooms are named `ROOM 1`, `ROOM 2`…, new storeys `FLOOR 3`, `FLOOR 4`…** A painted room
  must have a name, unique across the plan, because that is what a card is registered to.
  Obviously-provisional is what sends the host to the panel to say what it really is.
- **Room labels sit at each room's top-left cell.** The port's hand-tuned placements described one
  bungalow; a host who painted their own house got six labels in the wrong places.
- **The T chip is drawn as well as the count, not instead of it.** A room can hold cards *and* the
  terminal, and a chip that showed one would hide the other from the host about to go and find it.

- `model/.../InsiderBand.kt` — D-103's balance envelope, written down once. `of(seats)` is the
  band, `clamp(seats, chosen)` is the amendment: **both edges clamp the setting itself**, not just
  the draw, so a host cannot hand-pick a count outside it any more than UNKNOWN can land on one.
  The placeholder edges are the decision's own (`max(1, ⌊seats/6⌋)`..`⌈seats/4⌉`, integer-only —
  a band edge that depends on rounding mode differs between two phones). **Playtest owns the
  numbers, as it owns the 7**; nothing here chose one.
- `model/.../protocol/LobbyWire.kt` — the two bodies that ride inside a `TransportFrame.Carry`,
  and the guard that is the point of the file. `Handover(line)` goes **client → host** only;
  `Standing(joined, linesIn, insiders)` goes **host → client** and has no `String` in it at all.
  One body with a nullable `line` was the obvious shape and is the shape rule 3 forbids — a nulled
  field still exists and somebody makes it non-null later. As two types, *the lobby leaked a name*
  is not a bug that can be introduced by filling in a field.
- `platform/.../transport/LobbyDesk.kt` — the host's table, beside `SeatLedger` and for the same
  reason: pre-arm transport bookkeeping, no `Event`, no `Effect`, nothing that survives arming.
  It holds the lines **in memory with no store reachable from it**, drops a leaver's line with
  them (so `linesIn` can never exceed `joined`, which the gate depends on), clamps the Insider
  setting on every change *and* every time the seat count moves under it, and wipes every line on
  `roundEnded()`.
- `ui/.../Lobby.kt` — `HomeFinder` and `LobbyLink`, which are all `ui` is allowed to know about
  the radio (the same shape `HomeStore` gives it of the filesystem), the `MemoryHomeFinder` /
  `MemoryLobbyLink` fixtures behind every render, `OneLine`, and `LobbyModel` beside `PanelState`
  with the editor and the homes.
- `app/.../LobbyOverTransport.kt` — the one place mDNS and the websocket meet the screens.
  `NearbyHomes` over `HostBrowser`; `LobbyHouse` (ledger + desk + `TransportHost` + advertiser,
  bound on port 0 so the advertisement carries whichever port came up); `LobbyOverTransport`
  joining it as a client. Wired into `CheatRoot`, with discovery events landing in the 0.8 rig's
  log — mDNS failing is otherwise completely silent, which was the entire symptom on the first
  device run.

### The host joins its own house

HOST WITH THIS HOME brings the server up on that phone and then **joins it over loopback like
everybody else**. That is not ceremony: a host reading its own lobby straight out of its own desk
would have counts arriving by a route no other phone uses, and the route no other phone uses is
the one nobody tests. The host's Insider setting goes to the desk sitting next to it and the
answer comes back around the socket — the number the host reads arrived exactly the way the number
everybody else reads did.

### NO NAMES — what was removed, and what replaced it

The lobby drew six seat chips with names in them. They were a fixture, and reinstating them would
be a decision about what a client receives, which is not a call a lobby screen makes. What is
there instead is the same two integers drawn rather than written: one mark per seat, `linesIn` of
them filled. **The fill order is arithmetic, not identity** — this phone was told two integers and
could not order the marks by seat if it wanted to.

The guard is stronger than "the screen happens to have no names to draw", because the phone holds
two pieces of text that would be natural to put there: the resident name its owner typed, and its
owner's one line. `LobbyInputTest` renders the lobby with both set and asserts neither appears.

### The resident name does not leave the phone — a boundary, not an omission

Name entry is real typing and the name stays local. Nothing on the far side reads it: who is in a
round and what the house calls them is the loop's, and the loop is frozen. Handing player-
identifying text up to a host that has no lifecycle for it is a thing to be asked about rather
than started quietly, so it was not started. Wiring it is loop work.

### LIGHTS OUT — the boundary, stated explicitly

**The transition is presentation only.** The button is gated on `everyLineIn` — every seat here,
every line in, read off the house's counts and not off anything this phone decided — and when it
fires it walks *this* phone to the ARMED screen and does nothing else. It does not lock the seat
ledger, draw the Insider count, stamp the balance values into a recording, start a clock, or tell
another phone anything. Every one of those is arming; arming is the loop's; the loop is frozen. In
play this button becomes an intent the house answers by pushing a screen to everybody at once,
which is exactly why none of it may be decided on a device. `FlowModel.lightsOut`'s KDoc says the
same thing at the call site, where somebody will actually read it.

The gate is also the host's alone. A client is told *WAITING FOR THE HOST* rather than given a
button that would do nothing — the host turns the lights off, and everything after that is the
house answering rather than the host announcing.

### Two shared controls stopped lying

Both were found by a test that needed the truth and are worth knowing about beyond this unit:

### Decisions taken inside the design language, and flagged

1. **Signal bars are gone from the Join rows; the address is in their place.** mDNS resolves a
   name, an address and a port and reports no signal strength at all, so `///` would be an
   invention on the screen a player uses to pick which house they are walking into. The address is
   also the only true thing that separates two homes when both households have called theirs THE
   BUNGALOW — which is why the nearby list dedupes by address and port and never by name.
2. **`ReadoutField` grew a `hint`.** The design drew a blinking `Caret` in every field to promise
   the device was listening; a real field shows its caret only once focused, so an empty one reads
   as a box rather than as somewhere to type. YOUR NAME and `one line` are the two places it is
   used — both screens where somebody is being asked for something.
3. **The one-line field does not shout.** `transform` is identity there, against the field's usual
   uppercase: a house name is a label and a confession is a sentence.
4. **A blank line is refused and the screen stays put**, with the reason under the button — the
   same shape as a refused SAVE HOME, and for the same reason: walking away would leave the player
   believing the house holds something it does not, with the lobby's count agreeing with them.
5. **`0 OF 0 HANDED THEIRS OVER` is not drawn** while nobody has joined; the strip above already
   says NOBODY HAS JOINED YET in words.

## Unit 7 — notifications: one gesture, and what survives it

**Outcome: done, green, pushed.** Commit `d86b84c` — *"Everything that pops, and the one gesture
that takes it away"*. Full `./gradlew check`: **tests=814, failures=0, errors=0** across every
module and both targets, up from 797 — 17 new (`NotificationInputTest` 8, `NotificationsTest` 4 and
one `ScreenshotTest` viewer on desktop, `FlowTest` +2 on both desktop and `iosSimulatorArm64`).
Both lints clean,
`:ui:boundaryCheck` still reports `ui clean`, and `DeviceLayoutTest` stayed green throughout.

### What it is

D-105 deletes the read concept and replaces the whole surface with one idea: *everything that pops
is a notification, swipe up dismisses it, and that is the whole gesture vocabulary.* The port had
banners; it had no gesture, and the interesting half of D-105 — what is still there afterwards —
was not expressed anywhere.

- **`ui/.../Notifications.kt`** — `NotificationKind`, `Notification`, and the three the port draws.
  The load-bearing field is `NotificationKind.heldBy`: **where the thing survives once the banner
  is gone, or `null` for nowhere.** Messages holds every text, the Egress widget holds the
  countdown, and a house notice is held nowhere at all. It is a field rather than a paragraph
  because a paragraph cannot be tested and this is.
- **`NotificationBanner`** replaces `HouseBanner` and `EgressBanner` — one composable taking one
  piece of data, so there is no per-kind banner to write differently by accident. It tracks the
  finger the whole way up, springs back short of `SWIPE_DISMISS`, and only ever moves **up**: down
  is not a shorter swipe up, it is a second gesture, and this app has one.
- **`PanelFrame`'s overlay box now clips.** The banner moves, and the status row is the one thing
  on screen that must not — it is how a player confirms the perimeter is still armed while
  something is arriving. The banner slides *under* the boundary rather than over the clock.
- **`FlowModel.dismissNotification`** — the actions-layer edge, declared in `Flow.viaActions`
  beside HOLD TO DELETE and the other gestures no synthetic click can reach, and walked by
  `FlowTest`. **Nothing is written down**: not which notification it was, not that it was
  dismissed, not when.

### Three sentences that were each written twice are now written once

Every notification appears on two surfaces — the banner, and the thing that holds it afterwards —
and two copies of a sentence agree on the day they are written and never again.

| said twice | now |
|---|---|
| the house's text, on the banner and as the Messages preview | `Notifications.text.body`, and `NotificationKind.Text.heldBy` says why |
| UTILITY and LANDING, on the Egress alert and on the widget | `Notifications.EGRESS_NODES`, joined two ways |
| the house notice, on the meeting screen | `Notifications.notice.body`, drawn in exactly one place |

The Egress pair is the one that would have hurt. Containment needs two people at two separate
markers and **nobody may speak**, so the pair of room names *is* the coordination; a widget naming
a different pair from the alert would send two silent people to opposite ends of a dark house, and
it would look like a typo in the diff.

### "Stored nowhere" is proved by looking everywhere else

There is no notifications model, and that is the strongest form of the property — but it is also
unprovable by inspection, because the way this feature grows a memory is that somebody adds an
inbox, a history, a list-of-what-arrived, and the notice is caught on its way past. So
`NotificationsTest` renders **all 56 screens in both roles** and asks which ones have the notice's
words on them. The answer must be exactly `{Notice}`. A leak into Messages, Notes, a thread or a
tile fails there and is named.

Its converse is in the same file, because a rule that only ever says *fewer screens* is satisfied
by an app with no screens on it: the text banner **must** still be readable in Messages, and the
Egress nodes **must** be named on both the alert and the widget.

### D-105's other half: no read state, held by rendering rather than by lint

`noSurfaceCountsWhatHasBeenLookedAt` renders every screen in both roles and fails on UNREAD,
UNOPENED, UNSEEN, or NEW as a tag. The one count the design keeps is the backlog header — *N
MESSAGES* — which counts what is there rather than what has not been attended to.

**A vocabulary-lint stem was considered and rejected.** `VocabularyRules` matches stems by
containment, and `unread` is a substring of `unreadable`, which is `SavedHomesModel`'s own word for
a store it could not read. A stem there would have fired on four existing call sites and been
switched off within a week; and the words that would actually need catching (`isRead`, `readAt`)
cannot be matched by containment without also catching `thread`, `already`, `Reader` and
`spread`. Rendering catches the thing that matters — a mark a player can see — which the lint
never could.

### Decisions taken (minor, inside the established design language) — flagged, not silent

- **A swipe on a screen with no banner does nothing**, rather than navigating anyway. Both banner
  screens draw the *same* springboard underneath, so a dismissal that fired regardless would send
  a player who flicked at a tile back to page 1 from wherever they were — and it would be blamed
  on the touchscreen. `FlowTest` walks every screen in the game to hold it.
- **`SWIPE_DISMISS` is 14 design units.** Longer than a brush, shorter than a throw. Both failure
  directions are real in a dark room with one thumb: a dismiss that fires on contact throws away
  the one thing the house said, and a dismiss that needs a full swipe is a player fighting their
  phone. It is a presentation number, not a balance value.
- **The gesture tests read touch slop and the threshold out of the running composition** rather
  than hardcoding pixels. A hardcoded distance is a test that keeps passing while silently no
  longer exercising the branch it names.
- **`ScreenshotTest` gained a third viewer** — a banner at rest, mid-swipe and going, against a
  faked notch. The travel is a position, and a position is not a `ScreenId`, so the id-driven
  sweep structurally cannot reach it.

### Flagged for a person — not escalations, but somebody should look

- **The house notice still has a DISMISS button, and D-105 says the vocabulary is one gesture.**

  a phantom app"* and says swipe-up is the whole gesture vocabulary — which reads as though the
  notice should be a banner like the other two. Turning it into one restructures the meeting line,
  which is not a notifications change, so **the kind exists and its words are single-sourced, and
  the surface was left exactly as drawn.** Somebody should decide whether the notice is a screen
  the meeting stops at or a banner over the meeting.
- **The Perms screen lists a NOTIFICATIONS permission as PENDING.** That is the *operating
  system's* notification permission, and nothing in this unit is an OS notification — the phantom
  OS draws its own. Whether the real app ever asks for the system one (for a banner while
  backgrounded, say) is a question nobody has answered, and it is sitting on a screen telling every
  player it will be asked for.
- **`Notification.bodySize` is presentation on a data class.** It earns its place today because a
  headline and a sentence are set differently, but a data type carrying a font size is the sort of
  thing that grows a second one. Worth a look if a fourth kind appears.

### The finding the unit turned on

The brief asked for vote-selection echo and check-in echo. Reading the four meeting screens
together turned up something bigger: **I AM HERE, READY TO VOTE and LOCK IN look like three
different buttons and are the same button** — *one player says they are done* — and all three
navigated.

What follows each of them depends on **every** phone in the house:

| control | what it waits for | who can know that |
|---|---|---|
| I AM HERE | every living player *and* every out player standing at the meeting area (D-104) | the house |
| READY TO VOTE | a **unanimous** ready | the house |
| LOCK IN | the vote window closing and the ballot being read | the house |

A phone cannot count phones. So a press on any of them was the device predicting an outcome it
cannot see — the leak surface `ui ↛ core` exists to delete, arriving through the navigation layer
rather than through the rules. `Ghost2`'s I AM HERE was the same button a fourth time.

All four now echo and go nowhere. `ScreenGraph.exitsOf` is `emptySet()` for `Assemble`,
`Discussion`, `Vote` and `Ghost2`, and `Flow.autoAdvance` — which already carried every one of
those transitions — is the only way off them. Answering a ring stays an edge: a call is your own
phone's business.

### What is in the tree

- **`ui/.../Meeting.kt`** — `MeetingModel` beside `PanelState` with the editor, the homes and the
  lobby. It holds a check-in, a hand up, a selection and what was last handed over, and it can say
  nothing else. `locked` **compares** the selection with the handed-over vote rather than being a
  flag of its own: the design says the vote is *changeable until the clock ends*, so a flag would
  keep reading LOCKED IN over a row the house has never heard of.
- **`MeetingModel.meetingBegan()`**, called from `FlowModel.arrive` on any of the four ways into a
  meeting. A round holds several meetings and the model outlives all of them; a check-in from the
  first still lit at the second is a phone telling a player they are already standing where they
  have this second been told to walk.
- **`OutsideView` is a separate type, and that is the load-bearing part.** The living see a count;
  only a player outside the system sees who cast what (D-075). A living phone's meeting state is
  now *physically incapable* of carrying attribution — the same discipline as `Observation` vs
  `ObservationView`, applied to the ballot.
- **`ui/.../Countdown.kt`** — every clock is one value. The **window** is read off the auto-advance
  that ends it rather than restated, so a screen cannot say nine seconds and move after fifteen;
  the **position** arrives on `PanelState.secondsLeft`, with the design's drawn moment standing in
  until the house sends one, exactly as `arrivingAt` stands in for a missing cause. It fails
  closed: a screen not in the table has no clock even when the house sends a number for it.
- **Real tap targets.** `TAP_TARGET` is 36 design units — 44pt on the narrowest phone in range
  (375pt wide, scale 1.25, 35.2u, rounded up). The vote rows were 22u, about 27 points.

### ⚠️ THE TREATMENT — this is the thing to review

D-106 says a Subroutine's light signature is knowledge a player holds **in advance**, so that
Residents can plan a dark route. It was on none of the three surfaces it names. The sentence form
`SCREEN STAYS DARK` had been tried on the springboard widget and removed for how it read, and the
fact went out with the sentence.

**What is there now is a mark: three cells, one per rung of the ladder, lit up to this
Subroutine's own.** One cell for DARK, two for MEDIUM, three for BRIGHT.

```
   DARK  ▮        MEDIUM  ▮▮        BRIGHT  ▮▮▮
```

Four properties, and each is doing work rather than decorating:

1. **Unlit rungs are not drawn at all**, so the mark *emits in proportion to what it describes*.
   The bright mark is the brightest thing in its row and the dark mark is nearly nothing. On a
   screen whose governing rule is *minimise lit pixel area*, a legend spending the same light on
   every value would be saying "dark" in a bright voice.
2. **Constant width wherever it appears**, so on the work order it forms a column that reads DOWN
   the list. Choosing a route is a comparison between rows, and the last column is the only one
   that stays aligned when the names differ in length.
3. **Nothing on the ladder is zero cells.** The device has no off, and a phone emitting nothing is
   a phone whose player has been revoked — so an absent mark is not a value. That is what lets the
   two LOCKED rows, which name nothing on purpose, carry no mark without accidentally claiming
   their Subroutine is dark. Same fail-closed direction as the redaction schema: the failure mode
   is *my mark did not appear*, never *my mark said something I did not mean*.
4. **It is a mark, not a word, everywhere except one place.** The key at the foot of the work
   order is the only place in the game the mark is spelled out — on that screen because that is
   where the deciding happens, and not on the springboard, which is the screen you glance at while
   walking.

**Four surfaces. Three are D-106's; the fourth is mine and is flagged below.**

### ⚠️ THE TREATMENT — this is the thing to review

**Rule 8 says every Subroutine ships with its fake in the same change. The way that is true here
is that there is no fake.**

There is one screen per Subroutine and it takes no role. `SubroutineModel` has no `isFake`,
`PanelActions` passes no role into any of the three new actions, and none of the three screens
reads `PanelState.role`. An Insider's Handshake *is* a Resident's Handshake — one code path,
nothing to keep in step, and no state reachable by only one role. The distinction lives in the
authority's ledger and nowhere a player can stand.

That claim is checked in the strongest form available: **the two roles are rendered through the
identical input and compared pixel for pixel, at every step of the input** — not only at the end,
because a difference that opens halfway through a sequence and closes again is exactly what a
final-state check misses. Pixels rather than semantics, because the language here is four
luminance steps of amber with no hue: a tell in this game is far likelier to be a *shade* than a
word.

#### The second property, and the one that makes the first one hold

**Nothing on the device says whether you were right.** `SequenceEntry` and `ChoiceEntry` hold the
player's own input and **have no field for the answer to sit in** — rule 3's discipline applied to
adjudication rather than to redaction. A Subroutine's pattern arrives as an Effect, the screen
echoes taps, the entry returns as an Intent, and the *server* verifies it (D-042). D-043 is the
one documented exception to server authority and this is not a second one.

It matters more here than it looks. The moment a screen decides for itself that a sequence was
correct, a real Subroutine and a fake need two behaviours, and the difference is on a lit screen
in a dark room. Keeping the judgement on the authority is *what makes one screen serve both
roles.*

`ParityGrid` is the same move for the one Subroutine whose answer is on screen: the generator
flips one cell of a checkerboard and **discards the index before it returns**, so the screen
drawing the grid cannot mark the odd cell however carelessly it is later edited. The answer is not
secret — finding it is the work — but the *judgement* is not the phone's.

#### What is built, and what is honestly the house's

| | built | the house's |
|---|---|---|
| **HANDSHAKE** (dark) | the return: one large target, uniform cells lighting as beats go back | the buzzed rhythm (haptics, and no phone attached) |
| **REPLAY** (bright) | the entry: four dots, a tapped one lights and stays lit, cells count the sequence | the flash (a timed emission of light is an Effect, and the loop is frozen) |
| **PARITY CHECK** (bright) | **all of it.** No timed presentation exists, so the only thing outstanding is the verdict | the verdict |

#### Six treatment decisions, each flagged

1. **Handshake's beat cells are uniform, and this is a real fix.** The ported fixture drew its
   beats at *differing widths* — which reads as long beat, short beat: **the rhythm itself, on
   screen, in the one Subroutine whose whole instruction is *by feel*.** A player who can see the
   pattern is doing a bright Subroutine with the brightness turned down.
2. **Every numeral is gone.** `3 OF 5 RETURNED` and `FOUND 4 OF 7` both sat beside a display
   already carrying the same fact. The bench's own rule: *any subroutine showing a numeral is
   suspect — the display should carry the arithmetic, not the player.* Cells now do, in the same
   grammar as the light mark one row above them.
3. **STOP NOW, on all three, same words, same place, never changing.** Somebody walked in and the
   player is heads-down, lit or blind, both hands occupied, and needs out **without reading the
   screen**. In the dark that is muscle memory. It goes to the work order, where NOT THIS ONE
   goes from the screen before it. It is not a claim about the work — whether anything banked is
   the house's, and the button neither asks nor says.
4. **A sequence hands itself over on its last element; a single choice takes SUBMIT.** The rule is
   *an entry goes when it can no longer change*: a sequence's last beat ends it and a confirm
   would be a second gesture in the dark for no decision, while a single mark can move right up
   until it is sent. The second half is the vote's shape (`choice` / `handedOver` / `locked`) for
   the vote's reason — a mis-touch must not be final in a game whose input vocabulary is
   explicitly no twitch timing and no precise dragging.
5. **The motion budget row is now on all three** rather than on one. It calibrates on entry to
   *every* Subroutine (`gdd.md:928`). It is a readout and **not a draining bar**: there is no
   motion on a desktop render, and a bar animating on nothing — on the one client-adjudicated
   value in the game (D-043) — is the worst possible place for decorative movement.
6. **Parity's filled cells sit at `Amber.Mid`, over roughly 40% of the screen.** That is the
   brightest non-inverted screen in the game and it is deliberate — *a bright Subroutine makes you
   a beacon for its whole duration* is the point of having bright ones. **It is also the one value
   here that is a dark-room question rather than a screenshot one.**

- **`ScreenId.Sub` / `SubBright` → `SubHandshake` / `SubReplay` / `SubParity`.** The old pair was
  the light ladder used as an identity, and it stops describing anything at the third Subroutine.
  **One id per Subroutine is also what keeps the sweeps honest:** every guard in `:ui` walks

  `DeviceLayoutTest` never looked at. Cost: nine call sites.
- **The roster is data now** — `enum class Subroutine`, the design's ten with structure and light,
  and the work order and the springboard widget read their light off it. They were literals
  agreeing by hand, which is the arrangement D-106 already lost once. `CurrentSubroutine` no
  longer stores a name and a light beside a Subroutine that could contradict both.
- **`PanelVals.concealed` asks the roster rather than naming a screen.** It named `Sub`, so a
  second dark Subroutine would have shipped with a bright status bar over a screen built to emit

- **BEGIN moved to the actions layer.** Which Subroutine opens is a fact about the card that was
  read, so the screen cannot name a target — the same shape as a tap on the plan opening the room
  under the finger. `Flow.viaActions[ScanCaught]` is **derived from the roster**, so building a

### ⚠️ SHORT — the verification gap batch 1 named turned out to be closable

Batch 1's verdict was *SPECIFIED, blocked on verification*: **nothing is missing from the design**
(*hold N fingers for two seconds* is stated twice), but N-finger multi-touch held for a duration
"cannot be driven on a desktop render and this unit may not use a phone." The unit's instruction
was to attempt a phone-free route first and LOG-SKIP if none was genuine.

**A genuine one exists.** Compose's input injector addresses pointers **by id** —
`down(0, …) down(1, …) down(2, …)` puts three real pointers on the glass simultaneously — and
`mainClock` drives the two seconds in virtual time exactly as it already does for HOLD TO DELETE
(`HostSetupInputTest`, U4). The spike that settled it printed the pressed-counts the surface saw:
**`[1, 2, 3, 2]`** — three fingers arriving one at a time, then one lifted. Nothing is synthesised
at the semantics layer and nothing is a click; the hold surface publishes **no click action**, on
the principle `HoldToConfirm` already established (a hold a single synthetic click could fire
would not be a hold).

**What it still does not prove, and no desktop can:** that three fingers arranged on a six-inch
sheet of glass, in the dark, by somebody also watching a doorway, is a gesture a person can make.
That is a room test — and it is the same gap every Subroutine in this port has, so it is not a
reason to have skipped this one.

#### The property that actually mattered, and it is not "the hold works"

**The hold ends on the clock and never on the count.** A hold that completed when the *asked-for*
number of fingers arrived would be the phone grading the answer, and it would grade it **where a
player can see** — an Insider's fake hand sitting there while a Resident's right one goes. So the
two seconds run against whatever is on the glass, and the entry goes with whatever was there: one
finger, four, the wrong four. Exactly the property `SequenceEntry` has, which is that the entry
progresses identically whatever was entered.

That is held structurally, not by care: **`SHORT_FINGERS` lives on `SubroutineModel` and not on
`HoldEntry`.** An entry that knew the asked-for count could subtract it from the count it holds,
and then the phone is one careless edit from a verdict. It is rule 3 — *redact by constructing a
narrower type* — applied to adjudication. `SequenceEntry.length` is the one number any entry in
the file is told, and it has to be: a sequence has no other way to know it is over. A hold ends
on a clock, a converging shape has no finishing line, and a route ends when the player says it
does, so `HoldEntry`, `ScalarEntry` and `PathEntry` are told **nothing at all**.

### The four decisions this batch owned, each flagged

**1. Jam's two shapes: concentric rings converging in SIZE.** *(visual, mine, inside the design
language)* The input vocabulary at four luminance steps is position, shape, count, size, order,
presence, slow motion. Two rings closing on one another use **size alone**, sharing a centre — so
there is nothing to track across the screen, nothing that can leave the edge, and no direction to
confuse in the dark. It is also the version with the moment the design asks for (*slow, forgiving,
**satisfying***): at the eleventh press the two rings become **one ring**, which was rendered and
looked at specifically to check. The fixed ring is thicker and dimmer, the player's thinner and
brighter, so the coincidence reads as one ring with a halo rather than one ring eating the other.

**2. Jam's step size: a presentation fixture, 6 units, eleven presses from the opening to the
target.** *(placeholder, flagged as batch 1 flagged `HANDSHAKE_BEATS`)* How many presses the work
takes **is difficulty**, which is balance, which is out of scope. Nothing locks it at arming.

**3. Signal Trace's node layout: a 4×3 LATTICE, edges only between orthogonally adjacent cells.**
*(visual, mine, flagged prominently — batch 1 said node layout "is the entire legibility question
and the design says nothing")* At four luminance steps with no hue, a graph is read by **position**
and nothing else, and **edges that cross look like nodes that do not exist**. A lattice cannot
produce a crossing, cannot put two nodes close enough to read as one, and puts every node on a
grid a thumb can find without looking. Nodes are drawn at **36 units — `TAP_TARGET`** — because
the graph is the one screen in this game where two targets sit close enough that a near miss lands
on a neighbour. A force-directed layout would be a prettier Subroutine for a lit room.

**4. Signal Trace's decoy count: 3, a neutral fixture and NOT a tuning decision.** The design says
difficulty tunes by decoy count, which makes the real number balance. Three fills a 4×3 lattice
enough to make the route non-obvious and not so much that every cell is occupied.

**Plus one that is not a look-and-feel choice and is worth a sentence: Short has no progress bar.**
Two seconds of a bar growing is a two-second **ramp of light on the roster's darkest screen** — the
one built to keep a player invisible while they work. The screen says HOLD UNTIL IT RETURNS so the
player knows what they are waiting for, and what arrives is the same `RETURNED . WAITING` line the
other five use. The alternative (a row of cells filling in discrete steps, which would be in the
language and would not fade) was considered and rejected on the same ground.

### Rule 1, on the two screens where the tempting thing is a guard

**Signal Trace accepts a tap on a node its own graph does not join.** *Never early-return on
invalid in a client-visible path — the absent effect IS the leak.* A node that stopped answering
because it is not connected to the one you are standing on would be the screen telling a player
their move was illegal: the house's answer, arriving from the phone, on the only Subroutine where
the device would otherwise have an opinion about how the work is going. Every tap lands, the route
goes back, and **the house decides whether it was a route.**

**Walked nodes light; edges never do.** A walked *edge* lighting up is the same tell wearing a line
instead of a word — it would confirm that two nodes really are joined. The wiring is drawn at one
intensity throughout and only the nodes you touched change.

**Jam's SUBMIT is live from the first frame.** An inert SUBMIT would be the phone saying *the
opening position is not the answer*, which it has no way of knowing. Signal Trace's **is** inert on
an empty route, and the difference between the two is the whole distinction: **refusing to send
*nothing* is honest; refusing to send *this* is a verdict.** Both halves are now guarded.

### One structural change, and it is the one worth reviewing

**The roster dispatch is a single `when`, on `SubroutineModel`.** `FlowModel.tapSubroutine` and

### E8-1 · Does a vote reach the house when it is chosen, or when LOCK IN is pressed — DECIDED PROVISIONALLY, needs ratifying

**Raised by:** Unit 8, wiring the vote screen's rows and its button.

The design gives the vote screen **two** controls that both look like casting a vote — a row you
tap, and a button labelled LOCK IN — and never says what the difference between them is. Three of
the design's own statements have to hold at once and they constrain each other:

1. *Names plus Skip, **changeable until the clock ends*** (`gdd.md:412`, `:1006`).
2. *You see **how many** have voted, never what* (same lines) — so **having voted is a state**,
   distinct from having a finger on a row, or there would be nothing for that count to count.
3. *Not voting counts as a Skip* (D-075, reversing the GDD's "abstention").

**What was decided, because the screen could not be built without it:** the row is the selection
and LOCK IN is the hand-over. Tapping a name echoes and sends nothing; LOCK IN hands the current
selection to the house and the button reads LOCKED IN; changing the selection afterwards puts it
back to LOCK IN, because the vote is still changeable and the button must not claim the house is
holding a vote it has never heard of. LOCK IN is present and inert with nothing selected — not
voting is already a Skip and SKIP is already a row, so an empty hand-over would be a third way to
say the same thing and the only one with nothing on screen to show for it.

**What needs ruling.** Two questions, and the first is a what-a-client-receives call:

1. **Does the count `N OF 6 VOTED` count hand-overs or selections?** It is drawn today as the
   house's number and this phone does not move it, which is correct either way — but whether a
   selection alone should make you one of the N is a decision about what the house is told and
   when. If the answer is "selections", the phone is sending on every tap and the button is
   readiness rather than hand-over.
2. **Is LOCK IN a hand-over or a readiness signal?** It sits in the same position as the
   discussion's READY TO VOTE, which *is* readiness (unanimous skips ahead). If LOCK IN is also
   readiness, then a unanimous LOCK IN should close the vote window early, and nothing in the
   design says whether it does.

**Nothing was assumed about the outcome.** The button navigates nowhere and no count on the screen
moves when it is pressed; both answers are reachable from where the code now stands, and one of
them adds a field rather than undoing one.

### E7-1 · A swipe-up returns the panel from `NOTIFIED_DIM` to full — is undimming a client's to do? NOT DECIDED, gesture built

**Raised by:** Unit 7, building D-105's dismissal.

`NOTIFIED_DIM` is not styling and `Chrome.kt` already says so: a phone held as a lamp faces away
from its owner, so the drop in emitted light **is** the notification for anyone who cannot read
their own screen. It is world-observable, which is why the same note requires every banner to go to
everybody at once — a banner addressed to fewer than all players would be a beacon pointing at
whoever got it. And project rule 5 is that light is game state: the dim has to arrive as an
authored effect from the rules, as a step and never a fade, because *a ramp nobody authored is a
signal nobody authored*.

**The dismissal is the other end of that, and it is now a client-side gesture.** Before this unit
the port dimmed on `Notify` and not on `Home` and nothing moved between them; now a player's own
thumb does. Six phones dismiss at six different moments, so six lamps come back up at six different
moments, and that is a light change in a dark room that the house did not author.

**Why it was built rather than skipped.** The gesture is the unit, and there is no version of it
that leaves the light alone: a banner that stayed dimmed after being swiped away is equally a lamp
decision and a worse one. What is genuinely undecided is the mechanism, not the feature.

**What needs ruling:**

1. **Is dismissal local, or an intent the house answers?** D-097 already settled the shape of a
   refusal — the authority re-asserts the client's authorized view — and dismissal could ride the
   same mechanism: the phone asks, the house pushes the undimmed screen. That is the consistent
   answer and it costs a round trip on a gesture that must feel instant in the dark.
2. **If it is local, is the staggered undim acceptable?** The argument that it is: everybody got
   the same banner, so a phone brightening says only *"that player dismissed theirs"*, which is not
   alignment information and is not something an Insider can produce and a Resident cannot.
3. **Does the undim need to be a step rather than a fade** in the same way the dim does? It is a
   step today, because it is a screen change and nothing animates it. Nobody has said whether that
   is required or merely true.

**Nothing was assumed.** `NOTIFIED_DIM` itself is untouched — the dim is still exactly the

### E6-1 · Does the resident name leave the phone, and does any client ever receive another one — NOT DECIDED, skipped

**Raised by:** Unit 6, wiring the Join screen's name field.

The name field is real typing now and **the name stays on the device**. Nothing sends it anywhere,
because nothing on the far side reads it: who is in a round and what the house calls them is the
loop's, and the loop is frozen. Handing player-identifying text up to a host that has no lifecycle
for it — no place to keep it, no rule for when it is dropped — is how a promise gets made by
accident.

But the question behind it is not a wiring question and cannot be answered by a unit:

1. **Does the host learn names at all?** It plausibly must — a meeting has to be able to say who is
   being restrained, and the app is everybody's only light.
2. **If it does, does any client ever receive one?** That is a what-a-client-receives call. The
   design's lobby shows *counts*, and this unit held that line: `LobbyBody.Standing` is three
   integers and physically cannot name anybody. Widening it later means adding a field in a diff
   that says so, which is the property the two-type split was built for.
3. **If clients do receive names, when?** A name list in the lobby and a name list at a meeting are
   different disclosures. Occupancy is not public in this game — it is what the meeting is *for*.

**What is in the tree meanwhile:** `LobbyModel.residentName`, local, bounded, drawn only on the
screen it is typed on, and asserted absent from the lobby by `LobbyInputTest`. Nothing has to be
undone whichever way this goes; something has to be added.

### Unit 6's other client-boundary calls were the unit's own authorisation, recorded rather than raised

Three things cross to a client in this unit, and none of them was a judgement call made here:

- **The joined count and the handed-over count.** The design's lobby shows both — *6 JOINED*,
  *4 OF 6 HANDED THEIRS OVER* — and the unit brief names them explicitly. They are counts of a

- **The Insider setting, including UNKNOWN.** D-103: *the settings row may read UNKNOWN*. A chosen

  the host saying they are not choosing.
- **Nothing else.** In particular no seat identity, no name, and no line — and the type that goes

**Also worth a person's eye, not a ruling:**

  empty lambda), the same fault `RowButton` had and that this unit fixed there. Twenty-five call

- **The lobby is only reachable in playtest and debug.** The release variant root still draws the

### E5-1 · What payload marks the card as the Terminal — DECIDED PROVISIONALLY, needs ratifying

**Raised by:** Unit 5, building `TermTaken`, `TermRemove` and the `NoTerminal` gate.

The host-setup screens have always said *scan the card marked T* and *the T card is never an
ordinary marker*, and nothing in the design says how a scan tells one. A card's payload carries a
version, a shape and an id (D-069) and nothing else, so **the shape is the only field that can say
what kind of card this is**.

**What was decided, because the unit could not be built without it:** `t_shape` — literally the
letter T, which is what is printed on the card — is reserved. `MarkerShapes.TERMINAL` names it,
`MarkerShapes.registrable` is the other 43, `MarkerCard.isTerminal` reads it off the payload, and
`HouseMap` refuses it as an ordinary registration and refuses anything else as the terminal. The
roster still holds all 44 entries, because it is wire data and ids are never renumbered — this is
a shape that is spoken for, not one that was removed.

**Why it needs a ruling anyway.** It decides what is printed on paper and paper cannot be patched:
every card the QR sheet (4.11) ever prints is downstream of it. The alternatives not taken were a
reserved *id* prefix (invisible to a host holding the card, and the id is meant to be opaque) and
a reserved *version* (`CardPayload.decode` already refuses any version but 1, so it would mean
teaching the decoder that some refusals are not refusals).

**What needs ruling:** whether `t_shape` is the reservation, or whether the T card is marked some
other way — and, if the answer is yes, whether the three Array Wipe stations (Spares, Rack,
Disposal, the rest of story 4.5) are reserved the same way, which would take the ordinary roster
from 44 to 40 and is a decision about the shape set the legibility pass produced.


### E1-1 · The restrained player's takeover at the halfway mark of the vote results

**Raised by:** Unit 1, transcribing `Tally`'s LIGHTS OUT IN countdown.

D-102 (revision 20) ratifies: *"The restrained player's takeover appears at the halfway mark of
the vote results so they do not walk away when the countdown ends."* So `Tally` has **two**
outgoing transitions on the same countdown — everybody goes to the springboard when it ends, and

schedules. If it is the former, it is loop work and the freeze covers it; the note here is only so

### E1-2 · What a `Revoked` phone does when a meeting is called

**Raised by:** Unit 1, while establishing that `Revoked` has no way onward.

**What needs ruling:** nothing, if that is right — but it means a revoked phone in the current

Worth a deliberate confirmation that "a screen with nothing on it, waiting" is the intended
resting state for a player who is out, rather than something the design expects to move.

### E9-1 · The light signature has no home in the loop — it is a `ui` fixture, and it must arrive

**Raised by:** Unit 9, putting D-106's value on four screens.

`CurrentSubroutine.light` is a field on a `ui` fixture. In play the value has to arrive with the
Subroutine assignment like everything else, and **nothing was added to `PanelState`, to any
`Effect`, or to the redaction schema** — the loop is frozen and this is a what-a-client-receives
question, so it was built up to the boundary and stopped.

**What needs ruling, in the order it matters:**

1. **Does the signature ride with the assignment, or is it a static property of the Subroutine
   kind the client already knows?** They are different wires. If Sniff is *always* dark then the
   client can hold the roster and the house sends only which Subroutine — no new field, no schema
   row. If a particular instance can be lit differently from its kind, the value is per-assignment
   and needs to arrive.
2. **It is symmetric, which makes it the easy kind of disclosure and not a non-question.** Both
   roles see the same signature for the same Subroutine, so this is not a role-asymmetric leak.
   But every player's work order is their own, and *the set of signatures a player can see* is a
   statement about the work they were assigned. Whether that matters is somebody's call, not mine.
3. **Whether the rung is what the screen draws at, or only what it promises.** The mark says
   "three of three"; it does not say what luminance the real Parity Check renders. Rule 5 says the
   lamp is a pure function of state and no error path may touch it, so when the real Subroutines
   land from the diagnostics bench, *something* has to make the promise and the lamp agree. The
   enum's KDoc says explicitly that `rung` is a ladder position and not a brightness value, so
   nothing can start treating it as one by accident — but the two are supposed to correspond, and
   nothing yet enforces that they do.

Nothing was assumed. `LightSignature` lives in `:ui` beside the other fixtures, is not
`@Serializable`, and reaches no wire.

### E9-2 · `ARRAY WIPE` has no rated light signature and one is on screen

**Raised by:** Unit 9, drawing the work order's fifth row.

The GDD rates the **ten** roster Subroutines (3 bright / 4 medium / 3 dark, `gdd.md:565`). Array
Wipe is one of the two *structured* Subroutines and is rated nowhere.

**What is on screen today is BRIGHT, and it is this port's reading rather than the design's:** the

**Why it is here rather than in the flagged list.** Every other light value on screen is quoted

**Cheap either way:** one argument on one row.

### E10-1 · What does the house send back when a Subroutine entry lands — and is it the same thing for both roles?

**Raised by:** Unit 10 batch 1, building the three Subroutine screens up to the point where the
entry goes to the house and stopping there.

All three screens now end in the same place: the entry has been handed over, the screen reads
`RETURNED . WAITING`, and **nothing further happens**, because what happens next is an Effect and
the loop is frozen. That is the correct place to stop, and it leaves a question that has to be
answered before the loop is unfrozen rather than discovered while it is being written.

**A Resident's completion decrements System Integrity. An Insider's writes nothing (`gdd.md:382`).
Both phones must move anyway, identically, on the same schedule.** Three things follow and none of
them is settled:

1. **Does the client get a verdict at all?** Today it cannot form one — `SequenceEntry` and
   `ChoiceEntry` have no field for the answer. If the house sends *"that was wrong, go again"*,
   that is a new client-visible fact and it needs a schema row, and it needs to arrive for an
   Insider's fake in exactly the same shape and after exactly the same delay. **A fake that never
   fails is a role oracle after three attempts.**
2. **Or is a failure silent — a re-scan and nothing said?** Memory Dump's rule points this way:
   *a mismatch reports only "mismatch", never which elements were wrong* (`gdd.md:608`), and
   *failure → back to the source*. That is a design answer for one Subroutine, not for ten.
3. **Whichever it is, the Insider's copy has to be generated, not omitted.** This is rule 1's
   exact shape — the absent effect *is* the leak — and the design already names the pattern:
   *every player has a blackmail file and the Resident's is a fake* (`gdd.md:1421`).

**Why it is an escalation and not a flagged decision:** it is entirely *what a client receives*,
it needs a schema entry, and getting it wrong is silent and permanent. Nothing was guessed —
the screens simply stop at the boundary, and the honest state (`WAITING`) is on them.

**Cheap now, expensive later.** The three entry types are five lines each and hold no verdict; the
shape of the answer decides whether they gain a field or the screens gain a state.

### E10-2 · Does walking away from a Subroutine tell the house — and is that recorded?

**Raised by:** Unit 10 batch 1, giving all three Subroutine screens a STOP NOW.

Every Subroutine screen needs a way out that is not completion: *somebody walked in.* That is the
game — **stationary, glowing or blind, both hands occupied** is the vulnerability the whole design
rests on (pillar P4), and a Subroutine you cannot abandon would make it a trap rather than a risk.
So STOP NOW exists, and in the port it simply walks back to the work order.

**In play it cannot only be a navigation.** Two readings, and they are different games:

- **It is reported.** The house knows the Subroutine was opened and abandoned. Then *how often
  somebody bails* is data — and an Insider's fakes cost them nothing to abandon while a
  Resident's abandonment is real work lost, so a difference in abandonment behaviour is a read.
  Worse, if abandonment appears anywhere a player can see it (a meeting notice, a count), it is a
  channel nobody designed.
- **It is not reported.** Then an opened Subroutine stays open with a partial entry against it,
  and the next scan has to decide whether to resume or restart. Today the port restarts — which
  is a choice, made here, because a half-returned sequence from ten minutes ago is worse than
  nothing.

**Adjacent and unresolved: the same question is already open for carry state.** `GAP F-014`
(`gdd.md:1022`) asks whether a Memory Dump pattern or an Array Wipe disk survives a meeting, and
notes it matters twice over because the Array Wipe carry flag **blocks scanning every other
Subroutine**. An abandoned Subroutine and a carried disk are the same shape of question and
should probably get the same answer.

**Why it is an escalation:** it is what a client receives, what gets recorded, and — in the
reported reading — a behavioural channel that distinguishes a real Subroutine from a fake without
anybody putting it on a screen.

### E10-3 · A hold that goes with the wrong hand has spent the attempt — is one wrong answer the end of that Subroutine?

**Raised by:** Unit 10 batch 2, building SHORT.

Short is the first Subroutine where **the player can be finished before they know they were
wrong.** The hold ends on the clock, not on the count (which is the property that keeps one screen
serving both roles — see this batch's section), so a player with three fingers down where the house
asked for four hands over **three**, is told nothing, and finds the entry frozen. The only way to
try again is to walk away and scan the marker a second time.

The other five have the same shape but it is much easier to miss there, because their wrong
answers *feel* like answers: you tap four dots, you pick a cell, you walk a route. Short's wrong
answer is a hand that was slightly not what was asked for, in the dark, held for two seconds,
with no way to check.

**Three readings, and they are different games:**

1. **One attempt per scan.** What is built. Clean, and it makes Short genuinely tense — but it
   means the roster's *other short dark* — the safe quick one a Resident takes when they do not
   want to become a beacon — can silently cost a walk back to the marker.
2. **The house re-arms it.** The entry lands, the house says nothing a player can see, and the
   screen simply becomes ready again. Requires knowing what a client is told when an entry lands,
   which is **E10-1**.
3. **The phone re-arms it after some interval.** Rejected here without a ruling, because a screen
   that becomes ready again *on its own schedule* is the phone deciding an answer was finished
   with, which is adjacent to the phone deciding it was wrong.

**Why it is an escalation:** it is what a client receives after an entry lands, and it interacts
with balance — how often a Subroutine can be re-attempted is a difficulty number nobody has set.
It is the same ruling as **E10-1** seen from the player's side, and should probably be answered in
the same sitting.

### What the rework actually changed

U7 built the dim as *a banner is up*. D-118 says the dim is **not a notification style, it is a
two-member vocabulary**, and everything downstream of that had to move.

- **`NotificationKind` carries three rulings, one per field.** `heavy` (D-118: does the house dim —
  exactly two, and `FlowTest` names the pair rather than counting it), `stored` (D-119: does it
  stand on the lock screen until swiped), `heldBy` (D-105: where the thing survives). Five kinds
  now: `Opening` and `Egress` heavy; `Text`, `Unblocked` and `Notice` quiet.
- **`Notifications.arrivals`** replaces `onScreen`'s `when`: which notification is up, **what is
  underneath it** (a dismissal is a move to `Arrival.under`, not a constant `Home`), and **how it is
  presented** — a banner over the panel, or under the clock on the lock screen.
- **`PanelFrame` takes `dimmed` separately from `overlay`.** The two were one argument on the
  argument that they could then never drift apart; D-118 is precisely the ruling that they are
  different questions, so they are two arguments with one answer each and `Notifications.dims` is
  the only place the second is answered.
- **The quiet banner is drawn dark** — black ground, one hairline, the panel's own ink. *"No dim,
  no brightness spike"* is a claim about emitted light, and a quiet notification lighting a bright
  amber block would be the broadcast without the meaning.
- **`Quiet` does not buzz.** D-118 says quiet is *"nothing world-observable at all"*, and in a house
  where nobody may speak a buzz is about as world-observable as a phone gets. Six pockets going off
  at once is the same broadcast arriving through a different sense.
- **The ten seconds live in `Flow.autoAdvance`**, as three rows landing on each arrival's `under`.
  Reusing the house's own timing table means the undim is a transition of the whole panel rather
  than a timer inside a composable — and **`Quiet` has no row**, which the table's fail-closed
  default (a screen with no row waits) already gets right.
- **The lock screen shows what has arrived, under the clock.** Its two hardcoded messages became
  the two stored notifications, single-sourced with Messages; the arrival lands above them; swipe
  **left** takes any of them away. `NotificationsModel` holds what is still standing and can answer
  no other question — see the reflection guard below.

`Quiet` and `LockNotify` are new `ScreenId`s. Every sweep in this module walks `ScreenId.entries`,
so a quiet banner hiding behind a boolean on `Notify` would be a presentation none of them ever

### The lock screen's dim is the ground, not the glyphs

On a dark-field panel the dim is `alpha` over everything. On the lantern it cannot be: the amber
field **is** the emitted light, and fading black ink over a bright ground looks dimmer to the person
holding the phone and changes nothing in the room. So the ground drops to `NOTIFIED_DIM` and the
arriving notification stays at full amber — the one bright thing on a darkened screen.

  unit because of an exact-match.
- **The types** — `Notification`'s fields and `NotificationsModel`'s public surface are named in

### Decisions taken (minor, inside the established design language) — flagged, not silent

- **`Opening` and `Text` are two kinds, not one.** D-118 dims for *the house's opening text
  message*, and a second dim on the second text would spend the first one's meaning. So the opening
  text is heavy and every later one is quiet — which also gives the lock screen its second row and
  Messages its second house line.
- **The heavy two do not stand on the lock screen.** They clear themselves after ten seconds and
  what they were about is in its own home (Messages, the Egress widget); what stands under the
  clock is exactly the quiet stored ones. This is what keeps the design's lock screen at two rows.
- **Both house rows in Messages are drawn identically.** The older one dimmed would be
  indistinguishable from a read mark — the eye reads *bright above, faint below* as attended-to,

### What the wire looks like now

`Event.SubroutineCompleted` → **`Event.SubroutineReturned(at, actor, marker, entered: List<Int>)`**,
and the rename is the ruling rather than tidying. A client asserting a *completion* is a client
grading its own work, which is the one thing the whole Subroutine treatment keeps off the phone —
U10's entry types are physically incapable of holding the answer, so the phone could not honestly
send the old event even if it wanted to.

**One canonical entry shape for all six built Subroutines**, not a shape per Subroutine: an
element sequence, a chosen cell, a finger count, a signed offset and a walked route are all ordered
integers, and the *kind* of work is the client's presentation problem. Six wire types would have
been six chances to write a seventh that leaks.

**`Effect.SubroutineGraded(seat, accepted)`** is the answer. Two fields and deliberately no more —
no `remaining`, no *which elements were wrong*, no attempt count, no reason. A mismatch reports
only that it was a mismatch (`gdd.md:608`), and every extra field is one more thing that could be
generated differently for a fake.

**Redaction rows: `SUBROUTINE_GRADED to LIVING`** — both living classes, and addressed to the one
seat that returned the entry. Narrowing the row to either role is a one-word edit that reads like
a tightening and is a role oracle after a single Subroutine; broadcasting the effect would publish
who is completing work and how often, which is the per-player read the percentage-only meter
(D-103) exists to keep out of aggregate. Both are injected below.

### Rule 1 is the unit

The verdict is constructed **once, outside every branch**, exactly as the cooldown is in `contact`.
Five ways an entry can be refused — never scanned, scanned another card, returned at another card,
a seat the house assigned nothing, an entry already spent — each produce exactly one verdict of the
same shape. Every one of them is a place where `if (…) return Reduction(state, emptyList())` looks
like ordinary hygiene.

**The Insider's verdict is emitted, never omitted.** D-109's two rejected alternatives are both
worse than they look: *say nothing back about a fake* is rule 1's forbidden shape, and *roll a
plausible failure distribution* is a random number generator standing where the rule should be —
legible to the one reader D-047 never covered, somebody watching over a shoulder, who learns that
this player's work always works.

**The one asymmetry is server-side and appears on no screen.** A plain Resident's accepted entry
decrements SystemIntegrity; an Insider's writes nothing (`gdd.md:382`). `Effect.SubroutineGraded`
has no field that could carry it.

### ⚠️ D-130 came with it, and the reason is not tidiness

The meter total is now `seats × METER_PER_SEAT`, not `(seats − insiders) × 7`. Under D-103 the
Insider count can be hidden — drawn at arming, locked, told to nobody — and **a total built from
`seats − insiders` IS that count**, recoverable by division from any absolute meter value that ever
escapes. The percentage-only display rule closes the panel; this closes the arithmetic behind it.

Reachability now rests on **D-129's `K`** rather than on the operand: each Resident gets
`⌈M ÷ (seats − bandMax)⌉ + slack`, computed from public lobby facts only, so the Residents who
actually exist are never fewer than the number the order was sized against. Five per seat puts `K`
at 7 for an eight-seat home — where the old placeholder sat, deliberately, so the number moving is
a decision somebody makes rather than a side effect of this change. **Coefficient and slack are
playtest's** and are named as such. `K` itself is not built: it is drawn at arming, which is L3's.

`SystemIntegrityTest` was rewritten around the property rather than the number. Its sharpest
assertion is new: **arm the same home with every lawful Insider count and the bar must not move.**

### ⚠️ The differential harness told the truth, and four of its tests had to be rewritten

`with the Insider count fixed no effect depends on who the Insiders are` failed on the first run —
**exactly as its own KDoc predicted it would**, *"the day it fails, a rule has started branching on
identity rather than on population."* D-109 is that day, and the KDoc's instruction was *rewrite
this rather than deleting it.*

The meter genuinely depends on **which** seats hold the role, and it reaches players outside the
system, who see the real bars live (`gdd.md:1014`). So `METER_ASYMMETRY` names that one kind and
subtracts it from **both** runs before diffing. That is not loosening: one withheld line shifts
every later line, so a raw diff of two runs that banked at different moments reports lamps and
meetings as divergent too. Subtracting asks the question that is actually open — *is there anything
OTHER than the meter that depends on who the Insiders are?* — and it is paired with two
complements so nothing is hidden by being filtered:

- **the divergence is asserted to exist** (`without the subtraction the exchange is visibly dirty`),
  so the filter cannot go vacuous;
- **it is asserted to reach no living player** (`the meter asymmetry reaches no living player`).

The role-oracle test that replaces the two retired ones is **sharper than either**: every seat's
verdicts, filtered out of the per-client transcripts, must be byte-identical under a role exchange —
including the two seats that traded. A fake that never failed, a fake graded on a rolled
distribution, a verdict withheld, or a verdict a beat late all show up as a differing or missing
line. It runs on the fixture round and over 150 fuzz rounds.

**The fuzzer now scores sometimes.** It reads the opening entries off the rules and hands over what
was actually asked for about half the time, having scanned about half the time. Without that, the
graded-and-correct path — the only one that moves the meter, and the one D-109's whole asymmetry
hangs off — would never once have run, and every property above would have held over 150 rounds in
which the rule under test never fired. `the generator produces both verdicts` is the coverage
assertion that keeps it honest.

Recording format **4 → 5**: a version 4 recording holds an event this build cannot construct and a
state row missing the field a verdict is computed from. The state row now carries the work order
**answer key included** — omitting `expected` would leave the one piece of state a verdict is
computed from outside the replay guarantee, which is the `ended` omission again in a new costume.

### ⚠️ THE L3 BOUNDARY — what is spine and what L3 replaces

`GameState.openSubroutines` holds one `OpenSubroutine` per seat: the seat, what was asked, and the
marker it was armed at. **The shape is not provisional** — a seat has one open Subroutine, a scan
arms it, an entry spends it. Everything else is:

1. **Arming draws one placeholder entry per seat**, two integers over four values, derived from the
   round seed and the seat index. Seeded and recorded, and **it does not read `Role`** — which is
   load-bearing, not incidental: an Insider's fake order is drawn and sized by the same rule
   (D-129 makes order length role-independent on both axes), so a derivation that took the role
   would put the answer key itself on the role axis.
2. **That one Subroutine stays open all round, so the spine lets one assignment be completed
   repeatedly** — scan, return, scan, return. The meter is farmable without moving. This is the
   loudest thing L3 closes: advancing the work order is arming's job.
3. **Any card arms whatever that seat has open.** L3 resolves `(seat, card)` to *that player's*
   current Subroutine (D-123) and gives the other case D-124's `NOTHING FOR YOU HERE`.
4. **A re-scan re-arms the same question.** In play the house draws a fresh instance.
5. **`K` is not computed anywhere.** D-129's formula is written down in `SystemIntegrityTest`'s
   assertion and in `METER_PER_SEAT`'s KDoc, and drawn nowhere.

### Escalations

**E-L1-1 · Does an out player's live meter plus the presence plane add up to a role oracle? NOT
DECIDED, implemented the only way D-109 allows.**

D-109's asymmetry has to be visible somewhere, and where it is visible is the meter. Players
outside the system see the real bars live (`gdd.md:1014`), so they see a return that banked and a
return that did not — as an aggregate, with no attribution. **On its own that is nothing.** The
worry is D-111's presence plane feeding D-136's spectator map: a player who is out watches
*performing at room X* open and close, and then watches whether the bar moves. Over a round that is
a correlation, and the thing it correlates to is alignment.

Three things make it less than it looks and none of them closes it: the map is inference rather
than knowledge (D-136), two players can be performing at once, and the out cannot speak. But a
ghost who works out who the Insider is has learned the one fact the whole app is built not to tell
anybody, and *ghosts are in the room*.

What was implemented is the choice that leaks least of the two available: **`SubroutineProgressed`
is emitted only when the meter actually moves.** The alternative — emitting it on every return with
an unchanged value, which would have made the effect stream role-independent in *shape* and kept
the old differential tests passing untouched — is worse: it tells the out that *somebody returned
something that did not score*, which is a per-return count nobody designed and is E10-2's
behavioural channel arriving through the meter instead of through abandonment.

**Cheap now, expensive later**: if the answer is that the out must not see live progress at all,
that is one row of the allowlist. If it is that the presence plane must not feed a surface at the
same time as the meter, that is a design constraint on L3's spectator map, and it is much cheaper
to know before that map is built than after.

**E-L1-2 · A revoked player's correct entry still banks. NOT DECIDED, deliberately not guarded.**

`returned()` does not ask whether the actor is revoked, so a player who is out of the system can
still hand over an entry that moves the meter. In play their phone is on D-134's screen and has no
Subroutine open, so nothing can produce the event — but the *rules* accept it, and the fuzzer
proves it by doing exactly that. Adding `&& !state.isRevoked(actor)` is a state branch rather than
an effect branch and would be safe under rule 1 (the verdict is still emitted, and the seat is
`Out`, so the allowlist denies it either way). **It was not added because it was not asked for**,
and because per-seat round-state gating belongs with whatever answers F-005's other half —
orphaned Subroutines from a revoked player are meant to be auto-satisfied, which is the same
question from the other side.

**E-L1-3 · The two copy lines want a person.** Register ruled, wording drafted. See above.

### The work order, and the two types that keep it honest

`WorkOrder` holds `OrderEntry` rows with the answer key, the anchor and the dependency graph.
`asLines()` narrows to **`OrderLine`**, a sealed pair: `Known(index, subroutine, done)` and
**`Blocked(index)`** — a known unknown that is a *different type* rather than the same type with
its name nulled (rule 3). The order sent to a phone is always as long as the order the house holds,
because an order that shortened while work was blocked would make its own length a tell (D-114).

**`blockedBy` holds earlier indices only, so the graph is acyclic by construction** rather than by a
check somebody remembers to run. The only edge source ruled today is D-123's self-dependency: anchors
are handed out round-robin through a per-seat shuffle of the active set, so an order no deeper than
the home is a flat menu and an order deeper than it wraps, and the second visit to a card is blocked
by the first. **A dependency graph between *different* Subroutines is not drawn, because nothing
rules one** — the mechanism is general and only that one edge is populated.

**D-112 is why the light signature is not on the wire.** `model` gained `SubroutineKind` (the ten),
`ui`'s roster gained `kind`, and `Subroutine.of(kind)` is total — `SubroutineRosterTest` holds the
bijection in both directions, which is what makes it total rather than lucky. No field arrives, no
effect grows a member, the schema gains no row for light.

- **`WorkOrderIssued(seat, lines)` → `LIVING`, addressed to the one seat.** Both living classes and
  it must never be narrowed: an Insider's fake order is drawn by the same rule at the same length,
  so a row narrowed to Residents is a role oracle delivered by the allowlist — an Insider whose
  phone shows no work at all.
- **`OpeningMessage(seat, haptic)` → `LIVING + OUTSIDE`, one per seat.** A separate kind from
  `MessageDelivered` rather than a flag on it, for A2's reason: *a second dim on the second text
  would spend the first one's meaning*, and a flag is a thing somebody passes `true` for by

**It is emitted on every return, not only when it changed.** Sending it only when something moved
would make its *presence* a second verdict, arriving beside the real one and readable without being
told — rule 1's forbidden shape wearing an optimisation's clothes. Injection-verified below.

### The question moved onto the scan, and the answer moved with it

### The presence plane exists and reaches nobody

### The stub became the state, and both debts it named are paid

### The nodes are chosen above the rules, for `routeScan`'s reason

**`egressFor` sits beside `routeScan` and `armingFor`.** Choosing two markers in non-adjacent rooms
needs the home's *shape*, and the rules have never held house geography. Its answer rides

**Non-adjacency and the small-home degrade are ONE rule, not a rule and an exception.** The pair is

the degrade (D-127 floors a home at eight markers) — it is an Egress physically impossible to
contain that would look exactly like one that was not, and the Residents would lose to a shape they
could never see.

### The Sync Pulse: the phone hands over taps, the house decides everything else

**`Event.SyncPulseReturned` carries no node.** Where a player is standing is what the house recorded
when they *scanned* the node's card — D-136, *a performer's own scan is knowledge* — and a phone
naming its own node is the cheapest cheat in the game: sit on the couch, claim the Landing, contain
an Egress from a chair. `PerformanceEnded` carries no marker for the same reason and this is the
same decision. It carries no verdict either: a boolean *"I was on the beat"* is the phone grading
its own work.

The beat schedule is arithmetic on the tick the Egress fired at — **house-wide**, so concurrent
pairs forming a minute apart are still in time with each other, and nothing has to be recorded for a
replay to grade the same taps the same way. A pause does not move it.

**Five ways to fail, one shape for all of them.** Outside the system, not at a node, serving a
lockout, off the beat, on the beat with nobody opposite: every one produces
`Effect.SyncPulseAnswered(seat, held)`. Written the tempting way — silence unless the beat was good
— the absence reports *the house does not think you are at a node*, to a living phone, which is the
presence read D-111 split the two planes to close.

### ⚠️ The tap window exposed a constant that could not exist, and the tick rate had to be named

**`Balance`'s durations silently assumed one tick per second.** `REVOKE_COOLDOWN` was the bare
literal `60` for the lobby's `REVOKE COOLDOWN 60S`; nothing needed finer than a second, so nothing
noticed — and at `FixedTimestep.SUGGESTED_STEP_NANOS`'s 20 Hz that literal is a three-second
cooldown.

The Sync Pulse cannot be written at that floor **at all**: its tap window has to be a fraction of
its beat interval, and at one tick per second the only interval leaving room for a window is slower
than any heartbeat two people could catch.

**So `TICKS_PER_SECOND` is named, and it adopts 20 Hz.** `SUGGESTED_STEP_NANOS` is deliberately
named apart from `FixedTimestep.of` so that adopting it is a visible act; this is that act, and
**somebody should confirm it.** Every duration in `Balance` now reads through it, `REVOKE_COOLDOWN`
included — so the number on screen has not changed and the number in ticks has.

**The window has a hard edge and it is now guarded.** At half the beat interval every possible tap
is inside *some* beat's window and the grading stops grading: a player hammering the screen passes,
a player who never looked up passes, and the Sync Pulse is a four-tap button with a delay in front
of it. Nothing about that failure is visible — every test still passes and every pair still
contains. `Balance.syncPulseIsGradeable()` holds the line and `the tap window cannot swallow the
whole beat` asserts it, because the person who widens the window to be kind is not reading the KDoc
when they do it.

### One clock for the whole house, and it is not a `Cooldown`

The panel's *SHARED WITH THE OTHER INSIDER* is `GameState.egressReadyAt` — **one value, not a row
per seat.** `Cooldown` is keyed on a seat, so a house-wide clock held that way would be N rows that
must move together and *shared* would become a convention somebody maintains. It starts the round
already running at half (D-132), and no effect carries it: every phone learns the shared clock
restarted from `EgressOpened`, which every phone receives.

### The pause, and the arithmetic that is the whole of D-133

`deadline += (now − pausedAt)`. Fired at 60 with a 120-second timer, called in at 80 with the clock
reading 100, resumed ninety seconds later with the clock **still reading 100**. A reset would read
120, which makes the report a free Egress cancellation and every Egress ends the same way. Held
offers do not survive the meeting: everybody walked to the meeting area, and a beat held across it
would pair with somebody who has not been in the room for ninety seconds.

### Five effects, and the audiences are why there are five

| the fact | who gets it | why |
|---|---|---|
| `EgressOpened` | everyone, per seat | D-118's second dimming event; a dim reaching fewer than everyone is a beacon (D-076). Long haptic, D-135's first. **The actor included** — a phone that did not dim is the loudest tell in the design |

**A running Egress outlives its Insiders** (D-131): nothing in the type refers to who fired it and
nothing cancels it on a Restrain. That is a rule expressed as an absence, so it is asserted rather

### Escalations

1. ⚠️ **The tick rate is now decided in `Balance`, at 20 Hz, and nobody has confirmed it.**
   `FixedTimestep` refuses a default on purpose; `Balance` had one anyway, silently, at 1 Hz. This
   makes it explicit and adopts the architecture's suggestion. It changes `REVOKE_COOLDOWN` from 60
   ticks to 1200 — the same sixty seconds either way, but only if the rate is what this now says.
2. ⚠️ **`EGRESS_COOLDOWN` at 180s is invented.** Nothing in the design fixes it. `EGRESS_TIMER`'s
   120s is F-009's; the cooldown's is not, and playtest owns it.
3. ⚠️ **The Sync Pulse's shape is playtest's and is guessed:** a 1.5s beat, a 400ms window, a 2s
   lead, an 11-second run of four beats. Only *four taps* and *2–3s lockout* come from the design.
4. ⚠️ **F-009's better half is not built** — the app proposing the Egress timer from the painted
   grid, which is free because the longest marker-to-marker path is already computable. Nor is the
   lobby setting.
5. **An uncontained Egress does not end the round.** L6's, and stated above.
6. **Containment requires having scanned the node's card.** That falls out of D-136 and is the only
   way the house can know where somebody is — but nothing in the GDD says the node has to be
   scanned, and a player who walks to the right room and taps without scanning is told `held=false`
   with no explanation. Worth a design confirmation.
